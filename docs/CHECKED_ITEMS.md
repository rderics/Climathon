## 1. Latvian permits for collecting, storing, sorting and shredding EV traction batteries
**Status:** partly · **Confidence:** medium

A third-party pack hub needs a State Environmental Service (VVD) waste management permit before collecting, sorting or storing waste (Waste Management Law s.12). The s.12(6) exemptions (retail household collection, battery collection points under Reg 2023/1542 Arts 59-60, a holder's own short-term storage) probably do not cover a commercial hub. This is a reading by the researcher and was not confirmed with VVD. Fees under Cabinet Reg 703 are small: EUR 120 per activity, EUR 180 for two, EUR 240 for three or more, and EUR 70 for an amendment. Reg 703 sets no fixed decision deadline for a new permit. A mechanical shredding line is a B-category pollution permit (Annex 1: items 5.14 EEE shredding up to 75 t/day, 5.13 hazardous storage up to 1 year and up to 50 t, 5.12 hazardous storage over 1 year). A B permit replaces the separate Reg 703 permit. Annex 1 names no lithium-pack or black-mass line, so the correct category (5.14, 5.1 or A) is not found. The skeptic flagged that the researcher's idea that an intact pack under waste code 16 06 05 is non-hazardous (which would keep the hub below the hazardous-waste thresholds) is unverified and must not be used to plan around B-category. Black mass is reportedly being added to the EU hazardous-waste list (trade press, unconfirmed). The B-permit fee of EUR 362.83 comes from an old search snippet and is not usable. B-permit timing is not found (old texts suggest about 60 to 90 days, unverified).

**Key numbers**
- Reg 703 state fee: EUR 120 / 180 / 240 for one / two / three or more activities
- Amendment fee EUR 70, decision in 10 working days
- Trader/intermediary registration EUR 100, decided in 1 month
- B category thresholds: 50 t hazardous storage up to 1 year; shredding up to 75 t/day
- B-permit fee EUR 362.83: UNVERIFIED, old snippet, do not budget
- VVD financial security for a transport-only permit EUR 10,000/yr, hazardous multiplier 2.5: 2020 presentation, needs checking (costs angle)

**What it means for us:** Start with a Reg 703 collection/storage/sorting permit (EUR 120 to 240) and do NOT buy a shredder yet. Shredding triggers a B pollution permit with unknown cost and timing. Ask VVD in writing which category applies before any equipment spend.

**Sources:** https://likumi.lv/ta/id/236019-print (Reg 703, version of 09.12.2025, read 31.10.2026, official) · https://likumi.lv/ta/id/221378-atkritumu-apsaimniekosanas-likums (Waste Management Law, official) · https://likumi.lv/ta/id/222147 (Pollution Law Annex 1, official) · https://vvd.gov.lv/lv/media/7667/download (VVD presentation, 2020)

## 2. Fire rules for lithium battery storage
**Status:** partly · **Confidence:** low

No lithium-specific storage clause was found in Cabinet Reg 238 (Fire Safety Regulations, 19.04.2016). Coverage of the full text is fair but not complete, so absence is not proven. The page says Reg 238 loses force on 2 January 2027 and no successor was found. Only general or lead/alkali charging-room rules exist (separate ventilated rooms, 50% of extinguishers suitable for electrical equipment up to 1000 V). The building norm is LBN 201-15 (Reg 333 of 2015); LBN 201-10 is apparently superseded. Fire-compartment sizes in its Table 3 were not retrieved. Insurer requirements for battery storage in Latvia: not found. International insurers (TT Club, Sept 2026) want a fire-resistant dedicated area, early detection, sprinklers and quarantine of damaged stock, with no pricing. The VUGD statistic of 39 battery/accumulator fires in 2025 covers all batteries, not EV packs, and must not be used as an EV-pack fire rate.

**Key numbers**
- Reg 238 loses force 2 Jan 2027; successor not found
- At least 50% of extinguishers suitable for electrical equipment (cl.261)
- VUGD: 39 battery/accumulator fires in 2025 (all battery types)
- Li-ion type-90 storage cabinet EUR 7,000-11,000 (small cells/packs only, not 300-500 kg packs)

**What it means for us:** Fire is the one cost that can wipe us out and no premium is known. Do not sign a lease or accept any pack until a broker has quoted cover and a fire-risk assessment is done. Keep stock to a minimum (take packs only against a scheduled onward pickup).

**Sources:** https://likumi.lv/ta/id/281646-ugunsdrosibas-noteikumi (official) · https://likumi.lv/ta/id/301570-print (LBN 201-15, official) · https://lvportals.lv/dienaskartiba/387292-no-telefona-lidz-e-skuterim-ka-drosi-lietot-litija-jonu-baterijas-2026 (trade press)

## 3. Penalties for non-compliance
**Status:** answered · **Confidence:** medium

Waste Management Law Art.43 fines are in fine units of EUR 5 each (the unit value rests on one trade-press explainer, consistent with VVD's 2021 figures). Euro values below are the researcher's conversion. Handling without a permit: legal person EUR 250-2,800, natural person EUR 70-1,000. Breach of cross-border shipment rules: legal person EUR 300-4,000. Producer not registered: EUR 250-1,750. Waste accounting breach: EUR 250-1,250. The fines are small next to the cost of a fire, a revoked permit or a repatriated illegal shipment. Not found: Natural Resources Tax for batteries, EPR-specific fines, criminal liability, and fines under the EU shipment regulation.

**Key numbers**
- No permit, legal person: 50-560 units = EUR 250-2,800
- Illegal shipment, legal person: 60-800 units = EUR 300-4,000
- Unregistered producer: EUR 250-1,750
- 1 fine unit = EUR 5

**What it means for us:** Fines are not the main legal risk. The real exposure is a mis-classified shipment being treated as illegal (return-shipment costs, lost load) and permit revocation. Budget for compliance work, not for fines.

**Sources:** https://likumi.lv/ta/id/221378-atkritumu-apsaimniekosanas-likums (official; EUR conversion is our estimate) · https://lvportals.lv/skaidrojumi/309660-kas-ir-naudas-soda-vienibas-administrativas-atbildibas-likuma-2019 (trade press)

## 4. Producer registration and reporting
**Status:** partly · **Confidence:** medium

Cabinet Reg 331 (version of 1 May 2026, amended by Reg 227 of 28.04.2026) runs the battery producer register. The register holder is the Latvian Electrotechnical and Electronics Industry Association and the controller is the Ministry of Climate and Energy. Battery producers register before offering batteries on the Latvian market, with EUR 42.69 at registration and EUR 42.69 per year (invoice by 1 Feb, due 28 Feb). The registrar decides in 10 working days. The deadline for foreign producers to appoint a representative (15 June 2026) is already past. Reg 331 sets no registration duty for collectors, repurposers or recyclers. A waste manager can report on behalf of a producer under a contract (cl.32). NOT verified: whether a repurposer that sells a repackaged pack counts as a 'producer' under Reg 2023/1542 (it may, which would add EPR registration, conformity and liability duties). Under Reg 2023/1542 Art.60-62, secondary law-firm sources say EV batteries must be taken back free of charge by producers/distributors from 18 Aug 2025 (not checked against EUR-Lex).

**Key numbers**
- Registration EUR 42.69 + EUR 42.69 per calendar year
- Registrar decision 10 working days
- Free producer take-back of EV batteries from 18 Aug 2025 (secondary law-firm summaries)

**What it means for us:** Our own registration cost is trivial. The legal point that matters commercially is that the producer is obliged to fund take-back, so the hub should sell collection and treatment as a service to producers/importers/their scheme (a paid contract), not rely on owners paying. Selling second-life packs may make us a 'producer' with extra duties; settle this with a lawyer before the first sale.

**Sources:** https://m.likumi.lv/ta/id/267141 (Reg 331, official) · https://likumi.lv/ta/id/368032 (amending act, official)

## 5. Used versus waste status for cross-border moves (Reg 2023/1542, Annex XIV, DIWASS)
**Status:** partly · **Confidence:** low

A state-of-health test record alone does not make a pack 'used'. Annex XIV (read through a German-law mirror, single source, EUR-Lex text not obtained) requires for each shipment: an invoice/contract showing direct reuse and full functionality, per-battery test records, a holder declaration that nothing is waste, protective packing, a test record attached to the pack (ID, tester, date, results), and a transport document. Any missing item means the load is treated as waste and presumed an illegal shipment. The reference to Reg 1013/2006 is outdated: it is replaced by Reg 2024/1157 (most provisions from 21 May 2026), so the new cross-reference needs checking. Waste, shredded fractions and black mass ship as waste. Black mass classification (green list or notification) is not found; trade press (Recycling International, EUWID) reports it being classed as hazardous waste in the EU list, with no legal act confirmed. DIWASS: the researcher's claim that it is 'mandatory from 21 May 2026 for waste shipments' was contradicted. Sources show a phase-in: amber-list from 21 May 2026, green-list (Annex VII) from January 2027, and a transition to 31 Dec 2026 for some electronic duties. Latvia's DIWASS authority code LV01 (single source). Reg 2024/1157 rules on exports for recovery to non-OECD countries apply from 21 May 2027 (single source, ERA Malta).

**Key numbers**
- DIWASS start 21 May 2026 (phased; green list from Jan 2027)
- VVD notification-procedure fee EUR 142.29: purpose unconfirmed, not usable
- VVD contact for shipments: ap@vvd.gov.lv (VVD guidance PDF)

**What it means for us:** Treat every inbound pack as waste by default. Only a pack with the complete Annex XIV file may be sold as a product across a border (this is where the 30% second-life sale to Poland/Finland gets paperwork-heavy). Packs we cannot document go out as waste with a recycler who has the notification permit. Black mass to Finland/Poland is OECD/EU, so allowed, but expect hazardous-waste notification time and cost.

**Sources:** https://gesetze.legal/eu/vo_eu_2023_1542/anhang_xiv (mirror; verify on EUR-Lex) · https://mzp.gov.cz/system/files/2026-06/OVSS-Competent_authorities_responsible_for_registration_of_economic_operators_in_DIWASS-01062026.pdf (official, LV01) · https://pl.andersen.com/en/newsletter/mandatory-diwass-system-for-cross-border-waste-shipments-from-may-21-2026/ (trade press; use the URL as returned by search) · https://vvd.gov.lv/lv/media/7667/download (VVD guidance)

## 6. Who collects traction batteries in Latvia and the Baltics
**Status:** partly · **Confidence:** medium

No Latvian traction-battery recycler and no named Latvian collector were found; sorted batteries are sent abroad (lvportals 2023), and an Eco Lead plant at Kalnciems is stalled (LSM 2020, no current status). The Latvian compliance scheme with a published EV-battery tariff is SIA Latvijas Zalais punkts (LZP). Estonia has EES-Ringlus with a tariff. Lithuania: no tariff or collector found. Neighbouring benchmarks: Norway's importers' scheme ABR started on 1 Nov 2025 and forecasts more than 50,000 packs per year by about 2030 and over 100,000 by 2035; a peer-reviewed study found at least 14% of end-of-life Li-ion batteries stockpiled in Norway in 2024 (weak used-battery market, price-expectation mismatch, unclear responsibility for insurer write-offs). Finland: 52 full-electric cars recycled in 2024 (13 in 2023). Recycler capacity nearby: Fortum Ikaalinen about 3,000 t/yr (2021 figure, grant for 5,000 t/yr in 2023), AE Elemental Zawiercie up to 12,000 t/yr (opened Sept 2024). These are nameplate capacities, not proof they take third-party packs.

**Key numbers**
- Norway ABR forecast: above 50,000 packs/yr by about 2030
- Norway stockpiled at least 14% of end-of-life Li-ion batteries in 2024
- Finland: 52 EV cars recycled in 2024
- Fortum Ikaalinen about 3,000 t/yr (about 10,000 packs); AE Elemental Zawiercie up to 12,000 t/yr (about 28,000 packs)

**What it means for us:** There is a real gap for a local collector, but also proof that supply can outrun buyers (Norway stockpile). Nobody is publicly paying a collector. We must find out whether LZP and importers will contract a collector and at what price before assuming a revenue line.

## 8. Used-EV import volumes, fleet and model mix in Latvia
**Status:** answered · **Confidence:** high

Official fleet (CSB table TRC012, re-queried by the skeptic): 13,916 registered passenger EVs at end-2025, 9,234 in 2024 and 6,369 in 2023 (1.73% of cars; BEV/PHEV definition not stated). Also 469 electric lorries and 194 buses. Registrations 2025 (Uzladets, crediting Eleport, trade press): 3,505 used BEV and 1,646 new BEV (EU observatory counts 1,602 new BEV); used outnumber new about 2:1. H1 2026: 1,750 used and 843 new (EVShift, single secondary source). Top used models H1 2026: Tesla Model S 167, Model 3 139, Model Y 109, VW ID.4 83, Audi e-tron 55 65. Fleet is young, so retirements are tiny today. No EV scrap count exists: the researchers' guess of 'dozens to a few hundred' retired packs per year is unsourced and must stay labelled our estimate. The state subsidy was misreported in the findings. The corrected scheme (LSM 21 Apr 2026) is EUR 40m, EUR 3,000-9,000 per vehicle, with used cars at most 7 years old, at most 150,000 km and registered in Latvia for no more than 12 months, plus EUR 2,000 for scrapping an old car. The 12-month cap is enacted, not just discussed.

**Key numbers**
- 13,916 passenger EVs end-2025 (9,234 in 2024; 6,369 in 2023)
- 3,505 used BEV and 1,646 new BEV registered in 2025
- H1 2026: 1,750 used, 843 new
- Latvia ELVs collected 2024: 17,999 (no EV split)
- Subsidy EUR 40m, EUR 3,000-9,000 per vehicle

**What it means for us:** Latvia alone will not feed a hub for years: the stock is young and mostly under 5 years old. A Baltic or regional hub is needed for volume. The mix (Tesla, VW MEB, Audi e-tron) is NMC/NCA-heavy, which is the recyclable-value chemistry and the one buyers want.

## Risks that remain
- No source supports a guarantee that the hub 'cannot lose money'. The verified cost of disposing of an undamaged NMC pack at the one public tariff is about EUR 1.85/kg (about EUR 740 per 400 kg pack), while the Latvian producer fee is EUR 0.23/kg (about EUR 92). Profit exists only if a producer, importer or insurer signs a service fee that covers recycler quote plus freight plus ADR plus overhead plus margin. No such payer has been found.
- Take only fee-covered packs: the structural rule is to accept a pack only after a signed fee at least equal to a binding recycler quote plus freight plus ADR plus fixed-cost share plus margin. Free or speculative intake breaks the 'cannot lose' premise. LFP and damaged packs cost EUR 3.7-4.9/kg at GRS, so they need a much higher fee or must be refused.
- The 30% second-life revenue is unsupported: no verified European purchase price per kWh; Voltfang is moving to new cells and OEM batches; ENGYcell's funding is EUR 450k; new LFP cells cost about USD 65/kWh. Treat this share as zero profit until a signed purchase order exists.
- Own mechanical processing (copper, aluminium, steel, black mass) has no verified revenue data and a likely negative margin at small scale (HHL model about -EUR 1.90/kg; RWTH: unprofitable). It needs a B pollution permit of unknown cost and timing and carries a seven-figure equipment risk (Redux about EUR 3M).
- Fire: no Latvian lithium-specific rule found, but insurers may refuse or price cover prohibitively; no premium is known; one fire could end the company. Reg 238 loses force 2 Jan 2027 with no successor found.
- Legal status of a pack: missing one Annex XIV item turns a 'used' export into a presumed illegal waste shipment. Becoming a repurposer may make us a 'producer' under Reg 2023/1542. Black mass may be hazardous waste with notification duties; Reg 1013/2006 references are being replaced by Reg 2024/1157, and DIWASS has a phased start.
- Supply risk: Latvia has about 14k EVs, a young fleet, and no scrap statistics; the 'few hundred packs per year' guess is unsourced. Norway shows stockpiling when buyers lag. The enacted 12-month registration cap on subsidised used imports may slow the used flow.
- Competition for supply: producers' schemes (LZP and others) and the recycler's own take-back route (free for scheme members at GRS) may bypass us. Importers may already have contracts with foreign recyclers.
- Testing is a thin market: three Riga providers already on the same AVILOO kit at EUR 60-130, and the equipment is for sale to competitors. VAT status of those prices is unknown.
- Data quality: several figures are single-source or vendor claims (Voltfang 1/5, CommodityForge, bms-ev.com, evshop, Colliers snippet, LV01 code, EUR-Lex texts read via a German mirror). Fee conversions (SEK, DKK, fine units) are our own estimates. The costs-angle fire-safety item was truncated in the source text and the costs angle has no independent skeptic check.
