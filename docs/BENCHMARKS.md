# Benchmarks: tozero, Voltfang, Skeleton Technologies (31 Oct 2026)

Research by a multi-agent run (two researchers per company, one skeptic checking the key claims, one synthesis). Most press repeats company releases; treat yields, capacities, valuations and certifications as **claims** unless marked independent. Items the skeptic could not verify are listed under "do not cite".

## tozero (Munich) — relevance: medium (a possible black-mass buyer, not a peer)
**What it is:** a hydrometallurgical "second-step" recycler founded 2022. It takes **black mass** from mechanical recyclers and recovers lithium carbonate, graphite and a nickel-cobalt mix. It does **not** dismantle or shred packs; mechanical work is outsourced. Demo plant at Chemiepark Gendorf (opened 27 Mar 2026): about 500 t/yr of battery waste, over 100 t/yr lithium carbonate, leased building, mid-single-digit EUR million investment. Target: 45,000 t/yr plant by 2030 (earlier target was 90,000 t/yr by 2027). Sells to construction, ceramics and lubricants customers; aims for battery-grade later. [electrive, 27 Mar 2026](https://www.electrive.com/2026/03/27/tozero-opens-battery-recycling-demo-plant-in-upper-bavaria/)
**Money:** pre-seed EUR 3.5M (2022), seed EUR 11M (Nov 2024), EIC Accelerator grant EUR 2.5M (1 Dec 2024 to 30 Nov 2026) [CORDIS](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101189288). Company headline: EUR 17M raised (includes the grant). Gate fees, offtake prices and revenue: **not published**.
**What we can take**
- It confirms our split: mechanical step locally, hydromet by a specialist. Do not build hydromet.
- Copy the **joint-test pattern**: RET (mechanical, Bremerhaven) made black mass and tozero processed it under a March 2024 letter of intent, no financial terms [electrive](https://electrive.com/2024/03/23/german-battery-recyclers-ret-and-tozero-join-forces). Run a joint test batch with a named buyer before promising offtake.
- **Lease existing industrial space** instead of building (tozero's Gendorf site).
- Plan conservative prices: its disclosed customers are low-specification sectors.
- Present small, staged numbers: its own targets slipped.
**Do not copy / do not cite:** the 45,000 t/yr scale logic; "over 80% lithium recovery", "meets 2031 EU targets six years early" and CO2 claims (all company-sourced); inconsistent capacity claims (500 vs 1,500 t/yr); the 2023 Huayou feedstock deal (no volumes, no deliveries evidenced); the Sep-Oct 2026 leadership change (could not be verified). LFP handling is **not documented** anywhere I found.
**Questions to ask tozero:** would you buy black mass from a small mechanical processor in Latvia, at what minimum lot and pricing basis? gate fee or payment for NMC and for LFP black mass? black-mass specification (moisture, copper/aluminium contamination, particle size, discharge state)? would you run a joint test batch? who notifies cross-border waste shipment? is your 2026 equity round closed, and what is the actual throughput?

## Voltfang (Aachen) — relevance: high (closest comparable, and a warning on feedstock)
**What it is:** storage integrator building commercial and grid battery systems from requalified used EV modules, surplus new automotive modules ("New Life"), and in newer products new LFP cells. Revenue from selling systems, energy-management software and operations, not trading packs; arbitrage revenue goes to the investor-operator Palladio. [electrive, 22 Sep 2025](https://www.electrive.net/2025/09/22/voltfang-will-mit-investor-palladio-partners-selbst-grossspeicher-betreiben/)
**Money and scale:** Series B about EUR 15M (June 2025) with strategic investors including recycler Interzero and logistics/construction families (Fiege, Goldbeck) [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.de/2025/06/12/voltfang-schliesst-finanzierungsrunde-ab-und-eroeffnet-europas-groesste-second-life-batteriefabrik/); Series A included EUR 0.8M public grants. Produced 5 MWh (2023) and 20 MWh (2024), "still very small"; target 250 MWh by end 2026 (not shown to be met).
**What we can take**
- **Bundled take-back:** its system price includes end-of-life take-back of modules to partner recyclers (company FAQ). Offer ENGYcell the same guarantee.
- **Hedge the feedstock:** it mixes used EV packs, unused surplus modules and new cells. We should also accept OEM surplus, warranty returns and production scrap, not only end-of-life packs.
- **Funding mix:** grants plus strategic investors from the value chain (a recycler or logistics firm).
- **Certification stack** (company claims): ISO 9001 (TÜV SÜD), CE, UN 38.3; products UL 1973 / IEC 62619. It does not claim UL 1974 (repurposing); that could be our differentiator (to check).
- Our hub should supply graded modules, not operate storage.
**Do not copy / do not cite:** its loose grading story ("based on UN 38.3" is a transport test, not a state-of-health scheme; no thresholds or audit published); "over 80% residual capacity" (another source says 70-80%); "300+" vs "400+" systems; "up to 40% savings"; total funding USD 41.2M and credit facilities (single or unverified sources); price figures from an unopened PDF.
**Warning:** even with direct German OEM supply, Voltfang drifted toward surplus new modules and new LFP cells. With about 13,900 EVs in Latvia we cannot be a Latvia-only business.
**Questions to ask Voltfang:** what do you pay (or receive) per module or kWh for used packs and surplus modules, and do OEMs pay you to take them? your split of used vs surplus vs new cells and why used volumes disappointed? SoH threshold, test time and cost per module, third-party audited? how do you treat Regulation 2023/1542 for repurposed modules? who does your end-of-life take-back and at what cost? would you buy graded modules from a Baltic hub, in what volume?

## Skeleton Technologies (Estonia/Germany) — relevance: low
**What it is:** Estonian-founded maker of new ultracapacitors and a niobium-anode "SuperBattery", now pivoting toward AI data-centre power. No take-back, testing, second-life or Li-ion recycling activity found.
**What we can take (funding playbook only):** strategic corporate backer (Siemens), IPCEI state support (EUR 51M, reported 2021/2023; count once) [InnoEnergy](https://innoenergy.com/news-resources/skeleton-technologies-receives-an-additional-51-million-funding-to-scale-up-ultracapacitors-production/), EIB quasi-equity, EIT InnoEnergy as long-term investor since 2016. Idea (our inference, not sourced): ask EIT InnoEnergy for introductions and grants; a Baltic-founded battery firm needed hundreds of millions of euros for new-build hardware [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/132966-skeleton-technologies-raises-33m-in-pre-ipo-round-eyes-2027-us-listing/) while our lease-and-partner model needs far less.
**Do not use:** anything on its product or business model; USD 1B+ valuation and similar company-asserted figures; SuperBattery specs.
**Verdict:** probably not worth a meeting; at most a funding footnote.

## Cross-cutting lessons
1. **Feedstock is the binding constraint** (Voltfang). Plan a Baltic regional hub plus OEM surplus and production scrap; show a Latvia-only scenario next to a regional one.
2. **Nobody publishes the money flows.** No price per pack, kWh or tonne, no gate fee, no black-mass price, no OEM contract term appeared in any source. Our economics rest on our own assumptions and quotes, labelled as such.
3. **The mechanical/hydromet split is how the industry works.** The only supported black-mass buyer type is a hydromet specialist; LFP black mass has no evidenced buyer or price.
4. **Start-ups mix grants with strategic investors from the chain** (tozero: EIC, Honda, JGC; Voltfang: Interzero, Fiege, Goldbeck).
5. **Targets slip.** tozero went from 90,000 t/yr by 2027 to 45,000 t/yr by 2030. Pitch small, staged numbers.
6. **Regulation is a silent gap.** None of the three discusses Regulation 2023/1542, battery passport, repurposing responsibility or hazardous-waste permits; we must resolve this with the Latvian authority and a lawyer.
7. **Bundling end-of-life take-back into the price is a sales feature.**

## Changes this suggests to our plan
- Add a **fee line** (testing, grading, certification per pack) so revenue does not depend only on resale margin, which Voltfang's pivot suggests is fragile.
- Base the black-mass revenue case on **NMC/NCA only**; treat LFP black mass as zero or a cost until a buyer quotes.
- Get **written commitments**: a letter of intent or quote from a hydromet buyer (tozero or others) plus one joint test batch; an LOI from ENGYcell on volume and price.
- Offer ENGYcell a **take-back guarantee** for end-of-life modules.
- Build a **funding slide**: EU/Latvian grants plus a strategic investor from the chain; use tozero and Voltfang rounds only as examples, avoid unverified totals.
- **Certification plan:** ISO 9001 baseline, CE for any pack we place on the market, and check UL 1974 as a differentiator. Publish our own SoH grading scheme; do not cite UN 38.3 as grading.
- **Keep capex tiny:** lease existing industrial space; small mechanical line or outsourced dismantling.
