# Used EV pack supply reachable from Latvia, and the Baltic–Nordic second-life ecosystem (status 30 Oct 2026)

## Q1. Latvia's EV fleet (size, age) and when battery retirements start; Estonia and Lithuania equivalents

### Takeaway
At the end of 2025 Latvia had 13,916 battery-electric passenger cars (BEVs), 1.73% of its 802,784 cars (Statistics Latvia, data updated July 2026). The total was 2,106 at the end of 2021, and roughly half of today's stock arrived as used imports. Domestic pack retirements are therefore tiny today, probably tens to about 100 packs a year (inference), and are unlikely to become meaningful before the early 2030s. Lithuania has about twice Latvia's pool (29,035 BEVs on 1 Aug 2026) and Estonia about 60% of it (8,044 at end-2024). A Latvian venture would have to import most of its packs.

### Inferences
- **About half of Latvia's BEVs were imported used.**
  - In 2025 the BEV stock grew by 4,682 but only 1,612 new BEVs were registered. That leaves about 3,070 (about 66% of the growth) net from used imports minus deregistrations.
  - The same calculation gives about 1,605 (56%) for 2024 and about 908 (36%) for 2023.
  - Cumulative new BEV registrations for 2018–2025 total 6,595, against a stock of 13,916. So roughly half the stock entered Latvia as used cars, whose model years are older than their Latvian registration dates.
- **The fleet arrived recently.** About 85% of the end-2025 BEV stock was registered after end-2021 (13,916 − 2,106 = 11,810). Battery capacity on Latvian roads is roughly 0.6–0.8 GWh, assuming an average pack of 45–55 kWh (assumption, not sourced).
- **Order-of-magnitude retirement estimate (low confidence; it ignores PHEVs and differences in fleet age):**
  - Norway removed 4,570 high-voltage batteries in 2024 from a BEV stock of about 0.79 million. That stock is implied by SSB's 945,182 for 2025 and its 19.8% annual growth. The ratio is about 0.6% a year.
  - Applied to Latvia (13,916 BEVs), the ratio gives about 80 packs a year.
  - Applied to the whole Baltics (about 52,000 BEVs), it gives about 300 packs a year, roughly 10–15 MWh.
- **When retirements become meaningful.** Domestic retirements of 1,000 or more packs a year are unlikely before the early 2030s, when the 2022–2025 cohorts (about 11,800 cars, many already older imported model years) reach 8–12 years. Crash write-offs, warranty swaps and old imported Leafs, i3s and early Teslas supply a trickle earlier.
- **Sourcing consequence.** Supply for a grid-scale pilot of hundreds of kWh to MWh must be imported, mainly from Norway and Western Europe. Baltic domestic flows are better suited to small and behind-the-meter systems.
- **Lithuania as a regional source.** Lithuania's larger, fast-growing and used-import-heavy EV market is the nearest Baltic secondary source (inference from the registration data).

## Q2. Volumes and prices of retired or crash-damaged EV batteries in the Nordics and Europe (2025–2026), and who sells them

### Takeaway
Norway is the only nearby source at scale.
- **Fleet:** 945,182 EVs in 2025.
- **Removals:** 4,570 high-voltage batteries removed from scrapped cars in 2024, up 79%.
- **Outlook:** the importers' new collector, Autoretur Battery Recycling (operational since 1 Nov 2025), expects more than 50,000 batteries a year within about five years.
- **Stockpiling:** at least 14% of batteries in the end-of-life system were stockpiled in 2024 because demand for used batteries was weak.

Prices are opaque and widely spread:
- **Low end:** about US$25–75/kWh on some marketplaces, and roughly €30/kWh for an old Leaf pack on Finn.no.
- **High end:** €70–160/kWh for Tesla modules and packs in a 2026 vendor guide. That top range is on a par with *new* BEV packs, which averaged US$99/kWh in 2025 (BNEF).

### Inferences
- **Norway's real outflow is below forecast but rising fast.**
  - 4,570 packs in 2024, including small PHEV packs, is probably only about 0.15–0.25 GWh. That is below TØI's 0.6 GWh projection for 2025, which fits the "longer than expected" battery life.
  - ABR's projected 50,000 or more packs a year by about 2030 would be roughly 2.5 GWh, close to TØI's 2.1 GWh for 2030.
  - Even 1% of the 2030 Norwegian flow would exceed the whole Baltic domestic flow.
- **Stockpiles give buyers leverage.** Stockpiles, weak demand and unclear ownership of damaged packs suggest a buyer can negotiate lots from Norwegian dismantlers, insurers and Autoretur/ABR. ABR is recycling-oriented, though, so a reuse offtake would have to be negotiated rather than assumed.
- **Price ceiling.**
  - At €70–160/kWh, used packs from the vendor guide cost about as much as, or more than, new BEV packs at US$99/kWh.
  - Once testing, integration, power conversion and certification are added, second-life storage is only plausible if packs are bought well below about €40–50/kWh. Realistically that means distressed or stockpiled lots, older Leaf, i3 or e-Golf packs, or whole-pack reuse that avoids disassembly. This is an assumption-based threshold, not a sourced figure.
- **Cheaper Baltic sourcing.** The vendor ranks Latvia and Lithuania as cheaper sources than Norway (€70–105 against €110–160/kWh), so Baltic dismantlers and used-car importers may be worth testing as small local sources.
- **Finn.no price.** NOK 8,000 is about €680 at roughly 11.7 NOK/EUR (approximate rate, not sourced), which is about €28/kWh for a degraded 24 kWh Leaf pack.

## Q3. Rules for transporting and shipping used or damaged Li-ion batteries across borders, and the cost or delay they add

### Takeaway
- **Intact used packs** travel as ADR Class 9 dangerous goods (UN 3480).
- **Damaged or defective packs** must follow ADR special provision (SP) 376. Normally that means P908/LP904 packaging; packs liable to react dangerously ("unsafe") need P911/LP906 packaging or conditions set by a competent authority, with transport category 0, so no small-load exemption applies.
- **"Used" versus "waste"** is decided by evidence under Annex XIV of the EU Battery Regulation: a per-battery SoH test record, an invoice or contract showing direct reuse, a declaration that nothing in the load is waste, and protective packaging. Without that evidence the load is presumed to be an illegal waste shipment.
- **Waste batteries are now hazardous.** Waste lithium batteries are classed as hazardous under the updated EU List of Waste, whose codes become mandatory on 9 Nov 2026.
- **New shipment regime.** The new Waste Shipment Regulation has applied since 21 May 2026, with filings through the electronic DIWASS system. Norway had not yet implemented it as of June 2026.
- **Costs and delays found:**
  - ADR freight of about €250–700 per pack or pallet (vendor estimate).
  - Cargo insurance at 2–4% of value.
  - Per-pack SoH testing and special packaging for damaged packs.
  - A notification-and-consent procedure if packs are shipped as waste (time limits not verified).

### Cited Findings
**ADR (road transport of dangerous goods)**
- SP 376 covers damaged or defective lithium-ion, lithium-metal and sodium-ion cells and batteries, meaning those that no longer match the tested type under UN Manual of Tests and Criteria §38.3. Examples include leaking or vented batteries, batteries that cannot be diagnosed before shipping, and batteries with mechanical damage. Source: a guidance note by the Bavarian environment ministry and the Upper Palatinate trade inspectorate, dated April 2025 — [Bavarian SP 376 note](https://gewerbeaufsicht.bayern.de/gefahrenschutz/doc/merkblatt_sv376.pdf)
- **Standard damaged batteries (same note):**
  - Packaging must meet P908 or, for large packagings, LP904, and be performance-tested to Packing Group II.
  - Each battery is packed individually in non-combustible, non-conductive thermal insulation.
  - Packages holding a battery over 30 kg may contain only one, and LP904 large packagings are limited to a single battery or equipment item.
  - [Bavarian SP 376 note](https://gewerbeaufsicht.bayern.de/gefahrenschutz/doc/merkblatt_sv376.pdf)
- **"Unsafe" batteries (same note):**
  - These are batteries liable to disassemble rapidly, react dangerously, ignite or emit toxic or flammable gases.
  - They need P911/LP906 packaging tested to Packing Group I, or packaging and transport conditions set by the competent authority (in Germany, BAM, the federal materials testing institute; a copy of the approval must travel with the load).
  - Transport category 0 applies, so no exemption quantity is available.
  - Required wording: packages marked "DAMAGED/DEFECTIVE LITHIUM ION BATTERIES", and the transport document must state "TRANSPORT IN ACCORDANCE WITH SPECIAL PROVISION 376".
  - [Bavarian SP 376 note](https://gewerbeaufsicht.bayern.de/gefahrenschutz/doc/merkblatt_sv376.pdf); the same rules appear in the ADR 2023 text at [Certifico](https://www.certifico.com/merci-pericolose/documenti-merci-pericolose/documenti-riservati-trasporto-adr/batterie-al-litio-danneggiate-da-eliminare)
- SP 377 covers lithium batteries carried for disposal or recycling. They may be packed to P909 and marked "LITHIUM BATTERIES FOR DISPOSAL" or "...FOR RECYCLING". Damaged ones must still go under SP 376 — [Certifico (ADR 2023)](https://www.certifico.com/merci-pericolose/documenti-merci-pericolose/documenti-riservati-trasporto-adr/batterie-al-litio-danneggiate-da-eliminare)
- **Intact used packs (vendor guide, not authoritative):**
  - Shipped as UN 3480 Class 9; only ADR-certified carriers such as Schenker, DSV and Geis are suitable, not parcel services.
  - Pallet freight: €250–500 within Western Europe and €400–700 to Eastern EU; €350–600 per pack within the EU.
  - Specialised cargo insurance: 2–4% of declared value.
  - Fines of €2,000–5,000 per incident for unmarked shipments.
  - [bms-ev.com, May 2026](https://bms-ev.com/en/2026/05/08/source-used-tesla-battery-modules-europe-buyers-guide/)
- **Sea freight:** since a 25 Sep 2026 advisory, DHL Global Forwarding requires every battery in a consignment to be declared at booking. It may ask for safety data sheets, UN38.3 test summaries and packing photos, and missing documentation "may delay booking acceptance" — [DHL advisory](https://www.dhl.com/content/dam/dhl/global/dhl-global-forwarding/documents/pdf/dhl-dgf-customer%20advisory-mandatory-battery-declaration-25September2026.pdf)

**When a used battery is or is not waste: EU Battery Regulation 2023/1542**
- **Annex XIV minimum requirements for shipping used (non-waste) batteries:**
  1. A copy of the invoice and contract, or proof of transfer of ownership, showing the batteries are intended for direct reuse and are fully functional.
  2. Evidence of evaluation or testing for every battery: a SoH check plus a hazardous-substance assessment. A record must be attached to the battery or its packaging, readable without unpacking, giving the testing company's name and address, the tests performed, the results and the date.
  3. The holder's declaration that no material in the load is waste.
  4. Adequate protection against damage in transport.
  5. A transport document and a liability declaration for each load.
  - If the holder cannot provide these, the batteries are treated as waste and the load is presumed to be an illegal shipment. Warranty and repair returns between companies have exemptions.
  - [Annex XIV text](https://gesetze.legal/eu/vo_eu_2023_1542/anhang_xiv)
- Article 72(2): national authorities may check suspect shipments of used batteries. If a shipment turns out to be waste, the costs of analysis, inspection and storage can be charged to the producers or whoever arranged the shipment. The Commission may adopt a delegated act on "state of ageing" criteria for telling used and waste batteries apart — [Art. 72](https://gesetze.legal/eu/vo_eu_2023_1542/72)
- **Norwegian guide NEK VL 21:2025:**
  - Repurposing an EV battery for stationary use counts as a new placing on the market. The repurposer must confirm compliance in its declaration of conformity and carry out a new hazard and risk analysis.
  - IEC 62619 is the state-of-the-art standard for stationary batteries.
  - Module-level reuse needs technical module data and a redesigned protection circuit; cell-level reuse is generally not recommended.
  - Capacity should be tested on the DC side, for example at C/20 and 25 °C.
  - A separate NEK guide on dismantling and transport classification was still in development.
  - [NEK VL 21:2025](https://www.dsb.no/siteassets/rapporter-og-publikasjoner/nek/2025-nek-vl-21-gjenbruk-av-elbilbatterier.pdf)
- The EU battery passport becomes mandatory in February 2027 for EV, industrial (over 2 kWh) and light-means-of-transport batteries — [ESS News, 22 May 2025](https://www.ess-news.com/2025/05/22/falling-li-ion-battery-prices-mirror-solar-photovoltaics-trends-is-there-a-role-for-second-life-batteries-before-recycling/)

**Waste rules**
- The updated EU List of Waste classes lithium-based waste batteries and black mass as hazardous. The Commission says this means "better control of shipments", and that exporting hazardous waste from the EU to non-OECD countries is banned — [European Commission, 5 Mar 2025](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/battery-related-waste-codes-update-set-boost-circular-economy-2025-03-05_en)
- The update was published in the EU Official Journal on 20 May 2025 and entered into force on 9 Jun 2025, with an 18-month transition. The new codes become legally binding on 9 Nov 2026 — [EUWID](https://www.euwid-recycling.com/news/policy/new-battery-waste-codes-to-become-mandatory-from-november-2026-030625/)
- **Waste Shipment Regulation (EU) 2024/1157:**
  - Applies from 21 May 2026. DIWASS is used for "amber-list" waste (the category that needs prior notification and consent) from that date, and for "green-list" waste from January 2027.
  - Contracts must name the facility, waste codes, quantities and recovery operation.
  - Authorities may make at most three requests for additional information.
  - The Commission lists authorised non-OECD destinations by 21 Nov 2026, and exports elsewhere are banned from 21 May 2027.
  - [Abreu Advogados](https://abreuadvogados.com/en/conhecimento/publications/new-regulation-on-shipments-of-waste-what-changes-from-21-may-2026/)
- Norway had not implemented Regulation 2024/1157 into Norwegian law, because incorporation into the EEA Agreement is delayed (page updated 12 Jun 2026) — [Thommessen](https://www.thommessen.no/en/sustainability/regulation/eu-waste-shipment-regulation)

**Governance barriers named in regional research**
- TalTech's FinEst Centre (Shabbir and Krenjova-Cepilova, CONECT 2026 at Riga Technical University, published 8 May 2026) interviewed industry in Norway and Latvia. It names ambiguous battery classification, unclear liability and extended producer responsibility, weak safety supervision, lack of testing and certification capacity, and insurers' fire concerns. It concludes that targeted national measures are needed — [CONECT 2026 abstract](https://ect-journals.rtu.lv/conect/article/view/CONECT.2026.025)
- Thorne et al. (2026) report unclear responsibility in Norway for damaged batteries from insurance write-offs — [TRID](https://trid.trb.org/View/2762506)

### Inferences
- **Two sourcing routes with very different friction:**
  - **(a) "Used battery" purchase.** The seller or the buyer's agent tests each pack and labels it to Annex XIV, then ships under ADR as UN 3480. This adds testing cost per pack plus roughly €400–700 of freight per pack or pallet to the Baltics, plus insurance.
  - **(b) "Waste battery" purchase.** Crash-damaged, undiagnosable or untested packs bought from dismantlers or recyclers would normally fall here. It requires a permitted waste-treatment facility in Latvia, prior written notification and consent through DIWASS, contracts meeting WSR content rules, and SP 376 packaging (P911/LP906 for unsafe packs). That likely means weeks to months of lead time and a waste permit.
  - A startup pilot is far simpler on route (a). In practice that means doing SoH testing and Annex XIV labelling in Norway before dispatch, through a partner such as a dismantler, Stena or a test lab.
- **Hazardous classification from 9 Nov 2026.** Once the new codes apply, any Latvian facility accepting waste Li-ion packs will need permits and documents updated for hazardous codes.
- **Two regimes on the Norway–Latvia lane.** Norway's delayed adoption of the new WSR means the Norwegian authority still works under the old system while Latvia uses DIWASS. Expect friction on waste-route shipments from Norway.
- **Repurposer obligations.** Placing repurposed packs on the market makes the venture a "manufacturer". That brings a declaration of conformity, IEC 62619 or equivalent evidence, and probably battery-passport duties from Feb 2027. This compliance overhead is a fixed cost that hits small pilots hardest.

### Gaps

- Costs of P908/P911/LP904/LP906 packaging and of specialised transport for damaged EV packs were not found.
- Customs and VAT formalities for Norway-to-Latvia shipments (Norway is outside the EU customs union) were not researched.
- The practice of the Latvian State Environmental Service (VVD) on classifying used versus waste batteries and on hazardous-waste permits for repurposers was not researched.
- The status of the Commission's delegated act on "state of ageing" criteria under Art. 72(2) is unknown.
- Battery-passport obligations specific to repurposed batteries were not verified.
- No figures were found on how long ADR/WSR compliance actually delays shipments.

### Inferences
- **Best near-term fits:**
  1. **Norway Grants Green Business and Innovation (2027 calls).** It is Latvia-administered, involves Innovation Norway, which suits a Norwegian supply chain, and covers circular-economy and renewable technologies. The Q2 2027 call (€100k–500k) fits a pilot; the Q1 2027 call (€0.5–3M) fits scale-up.
  2. **EIT Urban Mobility (28 Jan 2027 cut-off).** It suits a pilot framed as second-life storage at an e-bus depot or charging hub with a city or transport operator as pilot host, for example Rīgas Satiksme. The 65% rate and the membership fee mean the consortium needs co-funding.
  3. **Horizon Europe.** Only realistic as a minor partner in a large consortium. The 2027 long-duration storage demonstration or recycling topics would need second-life written in as a work package.
- **Modernisation Fund energy-communities scheme.** A second round of this call (not confirmed) could fund small second-life storage paired with solar in an energy community, capped at €200k per project.
- **Interreg BSR.** Effectively unavailable until the post-2027 programme.
