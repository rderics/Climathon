Crash checks for insurers: pilot plan

Climathon 2026, Team 5 · work in progress · back to the 7 steps
Read this first. This is a pilot design, not a proven product. Facts below were checked against the original documents (section 6). The savings, volume and cost-per-check numbers are our estimates and can only be tested by running the pilot. No insurer has been contacted or named as a partner.

1. The idea in one paragraph

After a crash of an electric car, a trained technician follows a standard, non-destructive checklist within 24 hours of the tow and gives the insurer's claims handler a short advice on the traction battery. The goal: less tow, removal and quarantine cost, and fewer premature pack swaps or write-offs. We never say "safe"; we say "no gross damage found at the time of the test".

2. Why the numbers are small

  • In the Allianz study the battery was damaged in 2% of EV collision claims but caused about 7% of the claim cost. A full new pack cost €13,300–43,300; a casing swap €1,100–2,200.
  • Latvia: 35,617 accidents were reported to insurers in 2025. If EVs (about 1.6% of cars) are involved in proportion and one in ten is a "triggered" crash, that is about 57 checks a year across all insurers. One insurer would see only 2–3 live cases in 8 weeks.
  • So the pilot also reviews 20–40 past claim files and counts hybrids. It proves the process and the insurer's willingness to pay, not statistics.

3. The checklist (advice only)

  1. Event review: rollover, shifted mounting points, intrusion, airbags and pyro-fuse status.
  2. Hazard check: heat, smoke, leaks, need for quarantine.
  3. Visual check on a lift: casing dents, cracks, holes, water, heat on the casing.
  4. Data read: fault codes, charge level, cell voltages and temperatures, insulation values.
  5. Result: functional / repairable / damaged-defective / significantly damaged-critical. Per the Swiss guideline a dent of 10 mm or more, a torn casing or holes, water in the battery, or casing heat above 60 °C means the pack is critical and goes to disposal. The car maker's own repair rules always win.

4. Weeks and measures

WeeksWork
0–2Data-sharing and confidentiality agreement, GDPR terms, written checklist, qualified technician, reader and insulation tester, professional-liability quote, agree what counts as a triggered crash.
2–4Past-claims review: 20–40 anonymised EV and hybrid files, reviewed blind. Where would our checklist have changed the decision, and by how much?
3–9Live cases: every triggered EV claim checked within 24 h. The usual dealer or expert route runs in parallel as the reference. Follow cleared packs for at least 90 days where possible.
9–10Joint review, written report, decision on a paid phase.

We measure: time to decision, tow and quarantine days and cost, agreement with the dealer or expert verdict, cases where the decision would have changed, any false clear, our cost per check, and what the insurer would pay.

Price: free or at cost for the past-claims review, €0–100 for live cases, then test €150–250 per mobile check, or 10–15% of verified avoided cost. Suggested cash cap: about €6–8k (to confirm with quotes; equipment about €2–4k).

Main risks: liability if a cleared pack later burns (needs professional-indemnity cover first), the car maker overriding our advice, GDPR on claim data, falling quarantine costs, and delayed fires that no one-time check can rule out.
