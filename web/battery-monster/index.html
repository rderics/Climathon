Questions & Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you certify that an EV battery is 100% safe to drive?

No, and our legal wording explicitly avoids the word "safe". We report: "No gross mechanical, electrical, or thermal damage detected under test conditions at this date." Our verdict is a standardized technical advisory input for claims handlers and experts.

What if the manufacturer (OEM) mandates complete pack replacement?

OEM rules legally prevail and we do not attempt to override them. However, AZT/Allianz data demonstrates that in many cases, OEMs specifically allow casing/bracket swaps (€1,100–2,200 vs €13k–43k), and unnecessary quarantine can be eliminated with verified thermal/electrical data.

Where do batteries go when a vehicle is a total loss?

Healthy batteries from written-off vehicles are triaged, certified with Annex XIV test records, and routed directly to second-life energy storage partners (such as ENGYcell in Riga) to power EV fast-charging buffers and bus depots. Degraded packs are shipped via ADR transport to certified OECD recyclers (Fortum in Finland, AE Elemental in Poland).

Why is this better than building a battery recycling plant in Latvia?

Building a mechanical recycling line requires millions in capex and a VVD Category B pollution permit, while black mass in Europe currently has negative margins. Starting with mobile diagnostic triage requires just €2k–4k in equipment, is immediately profitable on Day 1, and secures the feedstock rights for second life.
